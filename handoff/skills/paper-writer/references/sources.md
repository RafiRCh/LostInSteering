# Sources and how conflicts were resolved

Sources:
- SPJ: Simon Peyton Jones, *How to Write a Great Research Paper* (Microsoft Research lecture). Covers one idea per paper, writing first as a forcing function, refutable and forward-referenced contributions, molehills rather than mountains, intuition first, no maze narration, related work late with generous credit, short conclusions, the four-sentence abstract (from Kent Beck), and guinea-pig readers.
- CIT: *How to Get More Citations* (YouTube). Covers narrative abstracts, gaps framed as painful problems, writing backwards from results, finding-stating titles, jargon, hype words, writing blocks and habits. The study statistics quoted in the video were not checked against the original papers, so the skill gives the advice without the numbers.
- HARD: *Why Your Research Paper Feels So Hard* (YouTube). Covers the figures-first order, the figure checklist, results as bullets turned into paragraphs, the competent-stranger methods test, the four discussion moves, the introduction funnel, the six-move abstract, and the title as the finding.
- Humanizer: github.com/blader/humanizer, MIT license. Fetched live at load time.

Conflicts:
| Topic | Sources | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| When to start | SPJ: before the research is done. CIT and HARD: from results. | Specification on day one, body from figures once results exist. |
| Introduction timing | SPJ: contributions early. CIT and HARD: introduction last. | Sketch early as the specification, finish late. |
| Related work position | SPJ: near the end. HARD: the funnel cites the field up front. | Brief citations in the introduction, full comparison late, unless the venue requires otherwise. |
| Title | SPJ: catchy. CIT and HARD: state the finding, no question titles, humor optional. | State the finding plus keywords. Humor only if the finding stays visible. |
| Abstract | SPJ: four sentences. HARD: six moves. | Six moves by default, four under tight limits. |

Left out as off-topic for writing: preprint and citation-boosting advice, and anecdotes. Writing-habit advice (named blocks, scheduled blocks, accountability) is in the human-readable guide, not in this skill, since it is aimed at the person.
