# Style

The humanizer (loaded live in Step 0) is the main rulebook for prose. These rules are specific to papers and add to it.

- Use plain words. If a smart non-specialist cannot follow the abstract, simplify it. One check: read the abstract into speech-to-text, and if it cannot recognize your words, readers will probably struggle with them too.
- Jargon in titles and abstracts is linked to lower citation counts (as reported in one source video). Define each term once at first use, then use it the same way throughout.
- Keep acronyms few. Most acronyms in the literature are rarely reused. Define each one at first use, and do not create new ones without a reason.
- Do not use hype words: novel, robust (in the figurative sense), unprecedented, innovative, groundbreaking, remarkable, state-of-the-art (unless it is a measured claim against a named benchmark). Describe what the result does.
- Use "we" for the authors' actions, and use the active voice where it shows who did what.
- Keep one term per concept. Do not switch between synonyms for variety in technical prose.
- Match hedges to the evidence. Do not stack qualifiers, and do not remove a hedge the data requires.
- Numbers: give the effect and its uncertainty, and the n, the first time a result appears.
- When defining a contested term, give your definition, with a forward pointer to related work for alternatives, instead of surveying definitions in the middle of the story.
