Text and Data Mining (T&DM) policy

Version dated July 2026. 

IOP Publishing Limited (“IOP Publishing/we”) recognise the importance of allowing researchers access to the metadata, full text and supplementary data of our content.  These data sources are a key resource in the research process.  We understand that researchers are becoming increasingly interested in mining the text and data included within that content and using it for AI purposes.

To ensure that the research community can access content without undue interruption or delay, and also to ensure that we provide agreed contractual levels of availability to our subscribers, our IOPscience platform blocks systematic downloading of content through a variety of methods.

Researchers seeking to obtain large amounts of data for AI and/or T&DM are asked to contact us (contentsupport@ioppublishing.org).  Subject to our review of the proposed use and any applicable terms, we may provide the requested content via SFTP or another agreed method.  In addition, to protect our investment, and the investment of the authors in our publications, the response to any request, and the terms and conditions governing any future use, will depend on the content to be mined and the purposes for which the AI and/or T&DM is being carried out.

IOP Publishing reserves the right to charge a nominal fee for supplying data such as full-text XML and PDF files for AI and/or T&DM purposes.  We do not charge for the supply of XML metadata, which includes only header, author, abstract and reference information.  Any AI and/or T&DM use of such metadata under this policy is permitted only for non-commercial purposes unless otherwise agreed in writing by IOP Publishing.

“Outputs” means the results of authorised AI and/or T&DM activities, excluding copies or substitute versions of the Publications.

These terms and conditions apply to both subscription content and open access content made available by IOP Publishing for AI and/or T&DM purposes, except to the extent that a Creative Commons licence or separate written agreement with IOP Publishing grants different rights, in which case those terms shall prevail.

Articles published under the Subscription model

IOP Publishing allows researchers access to our articles published under the subscription model on IOPscience for the purposes of non-commercial AI and/or T&DM, provided they, or their affiliated institution, have a valid subscription which provides them with access to all the content they wish to use for AI and/or T&DM.

Where access to content is governed by a current licence or other written agreement with IOP Publishing that contains AI and/or T&DM provisions, those contractual terms shall exclusively govern the relevant AI and/or T&DM activities and shall prevail over this policy to the extent of any inconsistency.

To avoid being blocked by the technical protections set up to prevent unauthorised systematic downloading of website content, individuals wishing to use content for AI and/or T&DM must email contentsupport@ioppublishing.org confirming:

  • their full name and contact details;
  • the name of any licensed institution to which they are affiliated;
  • details of the articles that they wish to use for AI and/or T&DM.  This can be specified as a list of DOIs, or as ranges of article publication dates within specific content;
  • the format in which they want to receive the content (XML or PDF).  Almost all articles have PDFs.  We have full-text XML for most recent articles but this is not available for some journals, such as Conference Series journals, or for many older articles.  You can tell whether an article has full-text XML by viewing the article page on IOPscience: if full-text XML is available for an article, the full text is visible in HTML form on the article page.  Where full-text XML is not available, XML contains only metadata/abstracts/references;
  • the approximate length of time that they want to have access to the data to carry out the AI and/or T&DM; and
  • the purpose for which they want to use the content for AI and/or T&DM. 

AI and/or T&DM for commercial purposes is not permitted unless expressly agreed in writing by IOP Publishing.

Articles published under the gold open access model

All use of articles or journals published under the gold open access model for AI and/or T&DM will be governed by the terms of the Creative Commons licence relating to the relevant article.

If the content you wish to use for AI and/or T&DM was published under the gold open access model under a CC BY licence, you have the right to use the content for AI and/or TDM purposes for both commercial and non-commercial usage, in accordance with the licence terms.

If the content you wish to use for AI and/or T&DM was published under any other Creative Commons licence, you have the right to use the content of the article for non-commercial AI and/or T&DM purposes only, in accordance with the licence terms.

Again, to avoid being blocked by the technical protections set up to prevent unauthorised systematic downloading of website content, individuals wishing to use content for AI and/or T&DM purposes must email contentsupport@ioppublishing.org confirming:

  • their full name and contact details;
  • the name of any licensed institution/organisation to which they are affiliated;
  • details of the content that they wish to use for AI and/or T&DM.  This can be specified as a list of DOIs, or as ranges of article publication dates within specific journals;
  • the format in which they want to receive the content (XML or PDF).   Almost all articles have PDFs.  We have full-text XML for most recent articles, but this is not available for some journals such as Conference Series journals, or for many older articles.  You can tell whether an article has full-text XML by viewing the article page on IOPscience: if full-text XML is available for an article, the full text is visible in HTML form on the article page.  Where full-text XML is not available, XML contains only metadata/abstracts/references; 
  • the approximate length of time that they want to have access to the data to carry out the T&DM; and 
  • the purpose for which they want to T&DM.
